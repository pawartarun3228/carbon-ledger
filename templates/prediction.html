Time-Series Experimental Methodology & Leakage Prevention
Chronological Rolling-Window Backtesting: Time-series data must never undergo random train/test shuffling. Models are evaluated via chronological rolling-window (expanding window) backtesting where each test step is predicted strictly using prior historical observations. Lag features (lag1, lag2, 3-month rolling mean) and cyclical calendar terms (sinmonth, cosmonth) are engineered exclusively from past intervals. Declared Model Selection Criterion: The best model is selected based on lowest Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) across identical chronological test folds.

Research Assumptions, Provenance & Scientific Limitations

  • Dataset Provenance: The demonstration inventory represents a university academic department facility (18 months, Jan 2023 – Jun 2024, ~1,800 m² floor area, ~120 occupants). The BDG2 empirical benchmark represents building Bear_education_Maryjane from the Building Data Genome 2 project (Miller et al., 2020), an 86,091 sq ft (7,998.1 m²) university higher education building with 24 months of sub-metered electricity readings (2016–2017).
  • Small Sample Variance: Short time series (< 24 months) exhibit elevated variance in autoregressive coefficients. Over small holdouts with low variance, residual sum of squares can exceed total sum of squares, mathematically causing negative R². Evaluating across a 12-month rolling cycle on the BDG2 benchmark demonstrates positive R² (up to ~0.49).
  • Exogenous Weather Drivers: Building cooling and heating loads depend heavily on local ambient degree-days (weather), occupancy schedules, and academic calendars which are not fully captured by autoregressive lag terms alone.
  • Scientific Disclaimer: Time-series forecasting models in this prototype are designed for comparative methodology study. Statistical extrapolation cannot substitute for certified engineering energy audits.

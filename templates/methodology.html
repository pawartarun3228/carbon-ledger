Scientific Methodology & System Architecture

Technical foundation, GHG Protocol accounting boundaries, emission factor provenance, and time-series machine learning experimental design.

1. Carbon Accounting vs. Machine Learning

A foundational design principle of Carbon Ledger is the rigorous separation between deterministic environmental accounting and probabilistic machine learning forecasting:

Dimension Carbon Accounting Engine Machine Learning Forecasting Lab
Paradigm Deterministic, physics-based stoichiometric inventory Statistical / non-linear pattern extrapolation
Governing Formula E = Σ (Activityi × Factori) ŷt = f(trendt, yt-1, yt-2, ȳ3, sinm, cosm)
Regulatory Role Official compliance, audited footprint, baseline setting Internal budgetary projections and scenario planning
Error Handling Zero-tolerance: rejects non-finite, negative, missing inputs Evaluated via holdout error metrics (MAE, RMSE)

2. System Boundaries & Scope Classification

Carbon Ledger implements standard corporate inventory boundaries under the Greenhouse Gas Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard:

  • Scope 1 (Direct Emissions): Direct emissions from fuel combustion owned or controlled by the reporting organization. Includes stationary backup diesel generators and department-owned vehicle petrol/diesel combustion.
  • Scope 2 (Indirect Grid Electricity): Emissions associated with the generation of purchased grid electricity consumed on facility premises. Calculated via location-based grid emission factors from the Central Electricity Authority (CEA) of India.
  • Scope 3 (Other Indirect Commuter Transit): Category 7 employee commuting and business travel, including passenger cars, municipal transit buses, and passenger rail transport.

3. Mathematical Formulations & Metrics

Carbon Accounting Equation:
Emissions (kg CO₂e) = Activity Quantity × Compatible Emission Factor
Net-Zero Target Allowance:
Target Allowance = Baseline × (1 − Target Reduction Fraction)
Forecasting Error Metrics:
MAE = (1 / n) × Σ | y_actual − y_pred |
RMSE = √ [ (1 / n) × Σ (y_actual − y_pred)² ]

4. Machine Learning Experimental Rigor

To maintain scientific defensibility, Carbon Ledger strictly forbids random train/test data splits for time-series modeling. Random shuffling destroys temporal order and creates catastrophic lookahead leakage.

Leakage Prevention Protocol:

  1. All monthly observations are sorted in strict chronological sequence.
  2. Lag features (lag1, lag2, 3-month rolling mean, and seasonal sine/cosine terms) are constructed using only prior temporal steps.
  3. Evaluation uses chronological rolling-window (expanding window) backtesting where each test step is predicted strictly using prior historical observations.
  4. Candidate models (Naive Persistence Baseline, Ridge Linear Regression, Random Forest Regressor) are compared across identical chronological test windows.

5. Scientific Limitations & Future Research

  • Exogenous Weather Covariates: Ambient temperature variations (cooling/heating degree-days) exert significant non-linear influence on electrical loads. Future work will integrate weather station telemetry.
  • Location-Based vs Market-Based Scope 2: The current prototype relies on location-based grid averages. Integration of power purchase agreements (PPAs) and green tariffs represents an essential next step.
  • Scope 3 Upstream Supply Chain: Embodied carbon in equipment procurement and food services requires hybrid input-output life cycle assessment (LCA) databases.

Approved Reference Standards

1. CEA CO₂ Baseline Database (v19) Central Electricity Authority, Ministry of Power, Government of India (Dec 2023). Factor: 0.716 kg CO₂e/kWh
2. IPCC 2006 Guidelines Volume 2 (Energy), Chapter 3 (Mobile Combustion) default values for motor gasoline & diesel oil. Petrol: 2.31 kg/L • Diesel: 2.68 kg/L
3. UK DEFRA / DESNZ 2023 Government greenhouse gas conversion factors for passenger vehicle and transit mobility. Bus: 0.089 kg/km • Rail: 0.041 kg/km
